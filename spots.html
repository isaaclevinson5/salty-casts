Where to Find Fish Along the Coast

Ninety percent of coastal predators hold in ten percent of the water. Focus your casts on areas where moving water pushes baitfish into ambush zones:

Rocky Points & Reef Edges

Currents accelerate around rocky headlands, creating eddies where sea bass wait to ambush disoriented baitfish.

Harbor Entrances & Breakwaters

Deep walls next to shallow sand flats act as highways for barracuda and jacks during dawn, dusk, and under harbor lights at night.

Sandy Beach Troughs

Look for darker, calmer channels between sandbars where waves stop breaking—predators patrol these troughs on a rising tide.

Timing the Tide & Shoreline Safety

  • The Golden Hours: The last two hours of an incoming tide and the first hour of an outgoing tide create the strongest water movement and feeding activity.
  • Never Turn Your Back on the Ocean: Watch wave sets for 10 minutes before stepping onto low volcanic or wet rocks, as rogue swell sets can surge unexpectedly.
  • Footwear Matters: Wear high-grip neoprene booties or felt/rubber-soled rock shoes on wet algae-covered ledges.
